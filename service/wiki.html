TRAINING & RECORDS

Every host holds a Service Permit

Every host at Flux must hold a valid OLCC Service Permit before working a shift. The permit satisfies the state's pre-service and first-week training requirements; the quarterly refresher below applies to all staff regardless.

Before your first shift
Obtain a valid OLCC Service Permit. This satisfies the required pre-service training.
Every 3 months
A refresher covering recognizing minors and visibly intoxicated guests, legal ID, spotting false ID, the checking-ID age, refusal methods, consequences, and house policy.
Following a violation
Any staff member involved in a sale to a minor or a visibly intoxicated guest must complete an Alcohol Server Education course within 45 days of notice.
HOW TO CHECK ID

Establish a routine and follow it consistently

Oregon law requires checking identification for any guest who appears under 30. Apply the same three steps to every check — touch, look, ask.

Step 1
Touch
  • Raised edges around the photo
  • Bumpy surfaces
  • Irregular lamination
  • Cuts, slits, or pin holes
  • Peeling lamination or uneven corners
  • Thickness & quality of the card
Step 2
Look
  • Check the expiration date first, and reject expired ID
  • Check the birth date
  • Does the photo look like this person?
  • Compare consistency of printing: letters/numbers that don't line up
  • Turn it over & check the back
Step 3
Ask
  • Ask for a second piece of ID. Fake IDs rarely come with backup
  • Ask their zip code, address, or middle name spelling
  • Ask what year they graduated high school
  • Have them sign their name & compare it to the ID

ACCEPTABLE ID

Only six forms of ID are valid on their own

Each must be physical, unexpired, and unaltered. A photograph of an ID on a phone is never acceptable, regardless of how official it appears.

Accepted alone
The six valid forms
  1. Driver license from a U.S. state, DC, or a Canadian province or territory.
  2. State-issued ID card with photo, name, date of birth & physical description (incl. DC, Puerto Rico, Guam, N. Mariana Islands, US Virgin Islands, American Samoa) or Canadian equivalent.
  3. ID card from a federally recognized Indigenous American tribe with photo, name, physical description & date of birth.
  4. Passport or passport card.
  5. U.S. military ID card.
  6. NEXUS or SENTRI card.

GOOD FAITH EFFORT

Cutting off a visibly intoxicated guest

Oregon law requires a good faith effort to remove a drink from any guest who is visibly intoxicated. This is satisfied by one of the following two actions:

Method 1
Place your hand on the drink
Physically take hold of the drink and try to remove it from the table or bar.
Method 2
Make a verbal request
Ask for the drink instead, if touching it could cause a disturbance.
VISIBLE INTOXICATION

The 50 signs of visible intoxication

A guest is visibly intoxicated when observation makes clear they have been drinking or using other drugs. You are not expected to determine a blood alcohol level. A single sign is rarely conclusive, but a combination of signs — or a marked change in behavior — is a strong indicator. When in doubt, do not serve.

Appearance
Bloodshot, glassy, or watery eyes Flushed face Droopy eyelids Blank stare or dazed look Twitching or tremors Disheveled clothing
Speech

HOUSE POLICY

The four required policies, and additional house practices

Every OLCC Responsible Vendor house policy must address the following four points:

1
When to check ID
Any guest who appears under 30. The OLCC minimum is 26; Flux sets a higher threshold as a precaution.
2
Accepted forms of ID
Only the six forms listed in the Acceptable ID tab. No exceptions and no digital copies. A guest who insists on presenting a digital ID may be an inspector conducting a compliance check.
3
How to check
The touch–look–ask routine in the Checking ID tab, applied to every guest without exception.
4
Consequences for selling to a minor
First offense: a warning and retraining. Second offense: termination.
House rule: two-drink limit while soaking
Guests may be served no more than two drinks while actively soaking in a hot pool or cold plunge. Additional drinks may be served only after a guest has finished soaking.
STANDARD PRACTICE AT FLUX

These practices apply to every host.

  • If you refuse a sale, management will support your decision without question.
  • All on-duty staff are informed when a guest has been cut off.
  • The visible-intoxication signs and the ID notice are posted where guests can see them.
  • Staff do not push drinks; non-alcoholic options are always offered.
  • Every cut-off, refusal, or ejection is logged and initialed by the staff member before the end of shift.
  • Intoxicated guests are helped to a safe way home — a rideshare or taxi, a ride from a sober friend or family member, or remaining on site until sober — rather than permitted to drive.
  • Drinks are opened and poured for the guest; sealed containers are not sold.
